2. Reach West Africa Play-ready. **Count taps** and note hesitation.

4. Back out to Africa. Back out to world/domain level.
5. Switch to Outlines, reach Southern Africa. *(Side B is Flags/Africa only — skip and note it.)*
6. **Repeat step 2 four more times**, as a returning learner would.
7. One-handed throughout.

**Step 6 carries the most weight.** Choreography that delights on the first traversal is the classic
thing that becomes friction on the fifteenth. If you only have time for part of this, do step 6.

## Read this before you judge

Three things worth holding in mind, because they are the ways this gate usually goes wrong.

**A negative result is a success.** Stopping #119 here costs one throwaway probe. Discovering the
same thing after the spherical asset pipeline, the renderer decision and the persistent scene costs
a great deal more. The plan names rejection as a graded outcome for exactly this reason.

**Judge the navigation, not the novelty.** The probe is deliberately plain — a stub Play screen, no
globe, Africa only. That is the point: if continuity only wins when it is also new and shiny, it
does not win.

**The probe has real limitations, and they are not evidence against it.** There is no 2D world
geography in Atlas, so its widest level is the Africa canvas rather than a globe; Play is a stub;
only Africa is covered. Discount those. Judge steps 2, 4 and 6 — reaching a scope, backing out of
it, and doing so repeatedly.
